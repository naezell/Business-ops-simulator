# Digital Twin — Architecture Decisions

Same format as `docs/decisions/` (ADR-001…008); these continue the numbering and
are indexed from `docs/decisions/README.md`.

## ADR-009 — Keep the daily time step; branch by replay
Status: accepted

**Context.** A digital twin suggests finer time resolution and the ability to fork a running simulation.

**Alternatives.** (a) Sub-daily discrete-event engine. (b) Snapshot/restore of engine state for branching. (c) Keep daily steps; reconstruct intra-day motion from logs; branch by re-running with dated actions.

**Decision.** (c). Within a day each crew's stops are already sequenced on a clock and logged; no mechanic needs cross-crew interaction inside a day. A one-year run costs about 40 ms, so replay from day 0 is cheaper than maintaining snapshot invariants. Counter-based randomness guarantees that a branch equals its parent before the first differing day (prefix identity, now tested).

**Consequences.** Playback is a visual reconstruction. Mid-day events (a truck failing at 11:00, emergency calls) are not represented; if a future industry needs them they enter as intra-day order arrivals in the dispatch loop.

## ADR-010 — Exogenous conditions are data on the scenario, separate from decisions
Status: accepted

**Context.** Stress tests need "the world changes" distinct from "the owner decides".

**Alternatives.** Editing company parameters for a stress run (loses time windows and pairing); a separate stress engine.

**Decision.** `Scenario.shocks`: time-windowed multipliers on named quantities plus forced outages, absences and weather. Shocks scale thresholds, never draws. The same mechanism with factor 0 provides the relaxations used for loss attribution.

**Consequences.** A strategy is always compared with a baseline under the same shocks. Shocks compose multiplicatively. `demand` scales visit frequency, not the customer count.

## ADR-011 — Reactive decisions are declarative rules, not code or AI
Status: accepted

**Context.** "Replace equipment when upkeep is excessive" and "hire when backlog persists" depend on simulated state.

**Alternatives.** Arbitrary callbacks in scenarios (not serializable, not reproducible across workers); a scripting language; an LLM agent in the loop (forbidden by the product requirements and non-deterministic).

**Decision.** `DecisionRule`: a conjunction of comparisons on a fixed list of observables read at the end of the previous day, with cooldown, firing cap and optional per-entity instantiation, triggering ordinary actions.

**Consequences.** Rules are data: they persist, travel to workers, and can be generated by the optimizer. Expressiveness is deliberately limited (no disjunction, no arithmetic); extend the observable list rather than the language.

## ADR-012 — Workload, pay, tenure and learning feed back into the workforce
Status: accepted

**Context.** Without feedback, overtime and long shifts are free and multi-year labor is static.

**Decision.** Fatigue from trailing weekly hours raises quit and absence probability and lowers productivity; quit hazard also depends on wage relative to a reference and on tenure; productivity follows a saturating learning curve; an annual real raise. All optional, all multiplicative on existing formulas.

**Consequences.** More illustrative parameters; all covered by assumption probes. `annualTurnoverRate` changes meaning slightly when dynamics are on (documented).

## ADR-013 — Long-term customer value is simulated, never a formula in the objective
Status: accepted

**Context.** A one-year horizon overstated price rises. A lifetime-value term in the objective would fix the ranking but would be a second model of customer behaviour inside the optimizer.

**Alternatives.** Terminal value = customers × CLV formula; always simulate many years; validate finalists over a long horizon.

**Decision.** Finalists are re-simulated over a long horizon; the score is the lower of the annualized in-horizon and long-run gains. Referrals, tenure-dependent churn and market saturation are added to the engine so the long run is meaningful. CLV may be displayed as a labelled estimate only.

**Consequences.** Validation costs simulation budget (bounded by the finalist count). Rankings among non-finalists still use the planning horizon alone.

## ADR-014 — Asset disposal, replacement and owner distributions enter the ledger
Status: accepted

**Context.** Multi-year runs showed depreciation running out, upkeep rising and cash accumulating without limit.

**Decision.** Disposal at salvage plus a share of book value above salvage, with a gain/loss income line and loan payoff; replacement = disposal + acquisition; optional monthly owner distribution above a cash level, recorded as an equity movement. Identities 4, 5 and 7 are amended accordingly.

**Consequences.** Two new cash-flow lines and one income line (result shape changes once, controlled by the golden-master procedure). Lease termination fees are not modelled.

## ADR-015 — The optimizer is a staged simulation study; no solver
Status: accepted

**Context.** The search space now includes timing, rules and combinations; findings must be validated against horizon effects, stress and uncertain assumptions.

**Alternatives.** MIP/LP or CP on a surrogate model; Bayesian or evolutionary search; fractional-factorial designs; staged screening with direct simulation.

**Decision.** Staged study: loss attribution by relaxation, capacity experiment, single-action screening, timing and price refinement, exhaustive pairwise interaction screening on a short list, beam search, then long-run, stress and assumption validation of finalists. Verdicts and a Pareto set instead of a single ranking number.

**Consequences.** Every reported number is a simulation result. The search is heuristic and says so. Cost is bounded by an explicit budget in run-units.

## ADR-016 — Parallel evaluation through a batch interface and a worker pool
Status: accepted

**Context.** A study needs on the order of a thousand runs and must not freeze the interface.

**Alternatives.** One worker running everything (simple, 3–4× slower); a server (cost, forbidden by requirements); WebAssembly rewrite (large effort, unproven gain).

**Decision.** The study orchestrator is async and reaches the simulator only through `EvaluateBatch`. In the browser a small pool of module workers implements it; in tests an in-process function does. Results must be independent of batching and worker count.

**Consequences.** Phase code is written batch-first (collect jobs, await, reduce). Determinism is preserved because each job is a pure function of (variant, seed).

## ADR-017 — Capacity and loss are measured by experiment
Status: accepted

**Context.** Bottleneck dollar figures were shares of lost revenue; capacity was not quantified at all.

**Decision.** Capacity = the largest customer-base scale that still meets the service constraint, found by simulating scaled customer bases; the gap between theoretical and achieved throughput is an exact accounting bridge from engine output. The value of each loss source is the simulated gain from removing it, reported as an upper bound.

**Consequences.** Both cost simulations (bounded). Figures describe the simulated period and the stated assumptions; the UI must say so.
